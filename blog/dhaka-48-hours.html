Dhaka on a Layover: A 48-Hour Local's Itinerary

5 min readUpdated 2026

Dhaka rarely makes anyone's shortlist of layover cities, which is exactly why it rewards the travellers who give it a proper look. Traffic is real and needs to be planned around, not fought — so this itinerary groups things by area rather than by "must-see" order, so you're not crossing the city back and forth.

Day 1 morning — Old Dhaka

Start early, before the heat and the crowds both build. Old Dhaka is dense, chaotic in the best way, and best explored on foot or by cycle-rickshaw once you're inside the old lanes. Lalbagh Fort is the anchor point — an unfinished 17th-century Mughal fort that's quiet and shaded even when the streets outside aren't. From there, it's a short ride to Ahsan Manzil, the pink palace on the Buriganga riverbank, worth seeing from the water if you can manage a short boat ride along the river.

Day 1 afternoon — Sadarghat & lunch

Sadarghat, Dhaka's river port, is one of the more overwhelming and memorable stops in the city — hundreds of wooden boats and constant motion. It's worth 30–45 minutes, not more, especially in the afternoon heat. For lunch, Old Dhaka's food is the real draw: kacchi biryani and street-side snacks are worth prioritising over a sit-down restaurant here.

Old Dhaka rewards a slow morning far more than a rushed full day.

Day 1 evening — Dhanmondi Lake

By evening, head to Dhanmondi. The lake area is where a lot of the city comes to walk, and it's the easiest way to see everyday Dhaka life without needing an itinerary at all — just walk the path, get tea from a stall, and take it slow.

Day 2 — Gulshan & Banani

The second day flips the pace entirely. Gulshan and Banani are Dhaka's more modern districts — quieter streets, better sidewalks, and most of the city's higher-end restaurants and cafés. This is a good day for a slower schedule: a proper breakfast, some shopping if that interests you, and an afternoon that doesn't require fighting through crowds. If you have any work calls to fit into a layover, this is the part of the city to be based in.

What to skip on a 48-hour trip

  • Anything requiring a day trip outside the city — Sundarbans, Sylhet, and similar destinations need more than 48 hours to be worth the travel time.
  • Trying to do Old Dhaka and Gulshan in the same day — the traffic between them can eat two or three hours you don't have.
  • Rushing Sadarghat — it's intense in short bursts; longer visits don't add much beyond the first 30 minutes.

Dhaka isn't a city that hands you its highlights easily, but two focused days — one slow and historic, one modern and easy — gives a fair sense of both sides of it.
