Renting a Car Abroad: What First-Time Renters Get Wrong

5 min readUpdated 2026

The advertised price on a car rental search is rarely the price you pay at the counter. Most of the gap isn't hidden fees so much as first-time renters not knowing which questions to ask before they book. Here's what tends to catch people out.

The deposit is often bigger than the rental

Rental companies place a hold on your card — sometimes $200, sometimes $1,500 — as a security deposit, separate from the rental cost itself. This hold can sit on your card for days or weeks after you return the car. If you're travelling on a card with a limited balance, or planning other big purchases during the trip, this catches people off guard more than any other single thing. Always check the deposit amount before booking, not after.

"Full insurance" rarely means full insurance

The insurance bundled into a rental price is usually the bare legal minimum, with a high excess (the amount you're liable for before insurance kicks in) still attached. Counter staff are often trained to upsell a second layer of insurance to bring that excess down — sometimes doubling the total cost of the rental. The alternative many experienced renters use is a standalone excess/deposit insurance policy bought separately in advance, which tends to be far cheaper than the counter upsell for the same coverage.

Almost every rental car complaint traces back to one of three things: the deposit, the insurance, or the fuel policy.

Fuel policy — the "full to empty" trap

Watch for a "full to empty" fuel policy, where you prepay for a full tank and are not refunded for unused fuel — you're effectively paying for fuel you don't use unless you can return the car nearly empty. "Like for like" (return it with the same fuel level you received it) is almost always the better deal for anyone not planning to drain the tank on purpose.

Photograph the car before you drive off

This one is simple and still the most skipped step. A slow video walk-around of the car — every panel, the wheels, the interior — before leaving the lot is the single best protection against a disputed damage charge later. Timestamp it, and don't rely on the rental company's own inspection sheet as your only record.

Quick checklist before you book

  • Confirm the deposit amount and whether it's a hold or an actual charge
  • Read what the included insurance excess actually is, not just whether insurance is "included"
  • Check the fuel policy — "like for like" over "full to empty" where possible
  • Confirm whether a second driver, GPS, or child seat costs extra before arriving
  • Photograph the car fully before driving away

None of this is complicated once you know to look for it — it's just easy to miss the first time, when the counter conversation moves fast and the upsells are the path of least resistance.
